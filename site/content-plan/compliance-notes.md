# Zin Choc: Internal Compliance Checklist

> **Internal document. Do not publish on the website.**
> This checklist is a practical to-do list for the owner. It is general information only and does not constitute legal advice. Confirm each item with the relevant regulator, your accountant or an Australian lawyer.

- [ ] Check whether you must register for GST (required once turnover reaches $75,000). The product copy states prices include GST; if you are not GST-registered, remove that wording and do not charge GST.

## 2. Food business registration and food safety (NSW)

- [ ] Notify or register your food business with your **local council** before trading. Home-based food businesses in NSW must notify council and may be inspected.
- [ ] Comply with the **NSW Food Act 2003** and the **FSANZ Food Standards Code**, including Standard 3.2.2 (food safety practices) and 3.2.3 (premises and equipment).
- [ ] Check current NSW requirements for a trained **Food Safety Supervisor**; requirements depend on the category of business.
- [ ] Confirm requirements with the NSW Food Authority for retail/manufacture of chocolate from your premises.
- [ ] Keep temperature and cleaning records; couriered chocolate should have a documented cold-chain practice matching what the shipping policy promises.

## 3. Labelling and allergens

- [ ] Food sold packaged must comply with Food Standards Code labelling requirements, including **Standard 1.2.3** (information requirements, warning and advisory statements, allergen declarations).
- [ ] Allergen declarations must follow **PEAL** (Plain English Allergen Labelling): required names (e.g. "milk", "soy", "tree nuts" by specific nut name, "gluten"), in bold, in a summary statement.
- [ ] Made-to-order goods sold direct may have different labelling obligations from retail-shelf goods, but you must still provide allergen information; keep the per-order ingredient/allergen card promised in the FAQ and Terms.
- [ ] Edible gold and silver: use only permitted food-grade materials (E175 gold, E174 silver) from suppliers who certify food grade.
- [ ] Alcohol in fillings: declare it, and check any threshold rules if alcohol content is significant.

## 4. Weights and measures

- [ ] If you sell by stated weight (e.g. the "45g" monogram bar), the **National Measurement Act 1960** and trade measurement rules apply: stated weights must be accurate, and packaging must show correct measurement markings. Verify scales are suitable, or remove specific weights from public copy if you do not want this obligation.

## 5. Privacy Act 1988

- [ ] Small businesses under $3M turnover are often exempt from the Privacy Act, but the website publishes a privacy policy and promises APP-consistent handling, so honour it: collect only what is needed, secure it, answer access/correction/deletion requests, and never sell data.
- [ ] Replace the placeholder privacy contact email (currently haem.z10@gmail.com) with a monitored business address.
- [ ] If you add analytics or marketing cookies later, update the privacy policy first (it currently promises no tracking cookies).

## 6. Australian Consumer Law

- [ ] The Terms of Sale acknowledge that ACL consumer guarantees cannot be excluded. Do not add "no refunds" signage or wording anywhere; blanket no-refund statements are unlawful.
- [ ] Change-of-mind restrictions on custom goods are acceptable; refusing remedies for faulty goods is not. Follow the 24-hour photo / replace-or-refund process in the shipping policy.
- [ ] Keep quotes, approvals and deposits in writing so the "custom made to your approved design" position is documented.

## 7. Spam Act 2003 (email marketing)

- [ ] Only send marketing emails to people who opted in. An enquiry about a wedding is not consent to marketing.
- [ ] Every marketing email must identify the business and include a working unsubscribe.
- [ ] If you add a newsletter signup to the site, make it a clear opt-in (no pre-ticked boxes) and keep records of consent.
